The science

Why one question beats rereading.

Katchy is built around a simple, well-studied idea: quick questions during a lecture keep your mind from drifting and help you remember more.

The study that inspired Katchy

In 2013, Harvard researchers Szpunar, Khan and Schacter had students watch a 21‑minute recorded lecture. Some got short quizzes between segments, others didn't. Then they asked students, at random moments, whether their mind was on the lecture.

41%of the time, students' minds wandered without the quizzes
19%with short quizzes during the lecture

The students who were quizzed also took more notes and did better on the final test. Read the paper (PNAS, 2013) ↗

The testing effect

Decades of research show that trying to remember something strengthens it more than reading it again. That's why every Katchy recap ends with a question, not just a summary.

What we're careful about

That study was run in a lab with a recorded online lecture and quizzes at fixed times. Katchy asks its question when you say you drifted, in a real classroom. We think it's a natural next step, but we're testing it with real students rather than assuming the same numbers.
